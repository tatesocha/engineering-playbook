Case study 3 · Business problem

Sort by consequence, not by author.

Low-risk changes merge on their own. High-risk ones wait for a person, and the workflow decides which is which.

The problem, in one line. Every pull request waited for a person, so the queue grew. Auto-merging everything would let a risky change land unseen.

Why it matters. A review queue that treats every change the same either slows everything or lets the dangerous change through. Sorting by consequence fixes both.

A real version of this

Business scenario · illustrative

A payments company's refund approvals

Small refunds were waiting in a manual queue all day. The fix was not to approve everything. It was to approve small, routine refunds automatically and send anything unusual to a person.

1Refund scored
2Under limit, routine
3Auto-approve
4Unusual or large
5Person decides

What we did

Low risk

Merges on its own

Once every required check passes.

High risk

Waits for a person

Migrations, access rules, deploy config, secrets, payments, lock files, or a change over 300 lines or 10 files.

Labeller

The workflow sets the label

The author cannot set it, so a risky change cannot be talked down.

Rules that come with it

  • No skipped checks. A failing test gets fixed, never disabled to get green.
  • No automatic high-risk merges. Ever.
  • Screens get walked. A change that touches a screen opens as a draft until someone has used it on a device.

The trade-off, stated plainly

  • Low-risk changes reach main with no human look, so the release review has to cover them.
  • Size thresholds are blunt. A large generated change can be high risk on size alone. That is intended.
  • A path rule can miss a risk. The list is reviewed whenever something slips through.
Why it matters, again. The review queue stops being one flat line. A person spends time where a mistake is expensive, and the rest moves at machine speed.

Where else this shows up

  • Payments operations, claims handling, content approvals, data pipelines.
  • Decision: sort by consequence, automate the bottom tier, and make the top tier impossible to self-certify.
  • Owner: the product lead sets the tiers. The workflow applies them.
