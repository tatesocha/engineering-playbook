Case study 5 · Business problem

Every new tool points at a gate that already exists.

Skills that cite existing checks do not drift. Spend gets measured before it gets cut.

The problem, in one line. The process grew faster than the team could check it. New tools were added before anyone ran them.

Why it matters. Each new tool is sound alone. Together they quietly multiply cost and create checks nobody runs. Growth without a rule is the usual failure mode for fast-moving teams.

What we did

Rule 1

Point, do not copy

Skills cite the gate they serve, so they cannot drift from it.

Rule 2

Measure, then cut

CI spend is sampled first, then cut in three narrow places.

Rule 3

Parallel work, own stack

Each agent gets its own worktree and its own port block.

Rules that come with it

  • Refuse when unclear. A script stops rather than guessing at state.
  • Credit upstream work. Third-party skills are named and installed under their own licences.
  • One bundle, named parts. A single command reports any upstream skill that is missing.

The trade-off, stated plainly

  • Many small skills are easier to check, but they add names to remember.
  • Cutting CI lanes risks a skipped check. The first real change after the cut confirms it.
  • Third-party tools change under you. Pinning and review stay manual.
Why it matters, again. Spend and risk are both decisions. Writing them down is what keeps a fast team from paying for checks it never runs.

Where else this shows up

  • Any team adding AI assistance faster than it can review it.
  • Decision: every new tool names the check it relies on.
  • Owner: whoever owns the budget signs off the cuts.
